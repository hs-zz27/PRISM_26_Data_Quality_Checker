These are development guidelines for checking supplied person polygons against their images.
A person means a real human visible in the scene, not a person shown on a poster or screen.
A person polygon should follow that person's visible boundary and include their visible head, hair or worn headwear, hands, fingers, feet, footwear and other visible body parts.
For partly occluded people, follow only visible portions and do not infer hidden body parts.
Small local omissions matter as well as large displacements, but do not invent a numeric acceptance tolerance.
If image resolution, blur, occlusion or an ambiguous boundary prevents a reliable decision, report uncertain and explain which region needs human review.
Judge each supplied annotation separately and explain the location of a suspected error. A correct region does not cancel an error elsewhere.
The reviewer controls the final decision and any correction.
